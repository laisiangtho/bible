## 1. Core Vocabulary & Loanwords

### Loanwords
Loanwords are words borrowed directly from other languages (predominantly English and Burmese) and phonetically adapted into Zolai orthography.

| Word | Origin | Original Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Soklek** | English | Chocolate | Chocolate |
| **Man** | Burmese | မှန် (*hpan*) | Right / Correct |
| **Bawlung / Bawhlung** | Burmese | ဘောလုံး (*baw:lum:*) | Football / Ball |
| **Padenpauh** | Burmese | ပြတင်းပေါက် (*pra.tang:pauk*) | Window |

## 2. Reduplication (Double Syllables)

Duplicating a word or syllable alters its function based on the context:

### A. Baby Talk / Kinship Terms
Repeating syllables often creates child-friendly words or forms affectionate terms for family members.
* **Na** = Hurt / Pain $\rightarrow$ **Nana** = Pain or injury (used when talking to toddlers, e.g., *"Nana maw?"* $\rightarrow$ *"Does it hurt?"*).
* **Pi** = Grandmother $\rightarrow$ **Pipi** = Grandma / Grandmother.
* **Pu** = Grandfather $\rightarrow$ **Pupu** = Grandpa / Grandfather.
* **Pa** = Father $\rightarrow$ **Papa** = Dad / Father.
* **Nu** = Mother $\rightarrow$ **Nunu** = Mom / Mother.

### B. Verb Reduplication (Continuous Aspect)
When a verb root is doubled, it indicates an ongoing or continuous action (**Present Continuous / -ing**).
* **Dam** (to be well/fine) $\rightarrow$ **Damdam** = Being well / recovering.
* **Nui** (to laugh) $\rightarrow$ **Nuinui** = Laughing.
* **Kap** (to cry) $\rightarrow$ **Kapkap** = Crying.
* **Pai** (to go/walk) $\rightarrow$ **Paipai** = Going / walking.
* **Leng** (to fly) $\rightarrow$ **Lengleng** = Flying *(Note: `lengleng` can also function as an adjective depending on context)*.

## 3. Prefixes & Suffixes

### Prefixes
Prefixes attach to the beginning of a word to alter or specify its meaning:
* **Hih~** (*This*): Specifies a proximal subject (*Hihnu* = This woman, *Hihpa* = This man).
* **A~** (*General/Neutral Marker*): Minimal semantic weight or structural prefix (*Adang* = Other, *Atuam* = Separate, *Amau* = They, *Ahihlo* = Otherwise).
* **Ki~**: Reflexive or reciprocal prefix.

### Suffixes

#### Plurals & Selection
* **~te**: Standard plural suffix for nouns (*Unaute* = Siblings, *Amaute* = They).
* **~teng**: Selective or specific plural suffix (*Amauteng* = They [specific group]).

#### Gender, Age, and Titles
* **~pa**: Masculine marker / Father (*Hihpa* = This man, *Napa* = Your father).
* **~nu**: Feminine marker / Mother (*Hihnu* = This woman, *Nanu* = Your mother).
* **~pi**: Senior feminine / Large entity marker (*Pipi* = Grandmother, *Nupi* = Elder/married female).
* **~pu**: Senior masculine marker (*Papi* = Respected elder male, *Napu* = Your grandfather).

#### Aspect, Tense, and Modal Verbal Suffixes
* **~ta**: Inchoative / immediate action (*Paita* = Going now, *Neita* = Eating now).
* **~khin**: Completed action (*Paikhin* = Already gone, *Neikhin* = Already eaten).
* **~khinta**: Perfective aspect (*Paikhinta* = Has already left completely).
* **~sa**: Past state / resultative (*Bawlsa* = Already made/prepared).
* **~theih**: Ability / capability (*Guihtheih* = Intoxicating, *Nektheih* = Edible).
* **~zaw**: Comparative (*Hoihzaw* = Better, *Lianzaw* = Bigger).

## 4. Creature & Human Base Words & Suffix Morphology

Applying specific suffixes to base nouns for living creatures—including both humans (e.g., **Mi** = *Person/Human*) and animals (e.g., **Tuu** = *Sheep*)—explicitly defines age, sex, usage, or group organization:

| Base Word | Suffix | Combined Form | English Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tuu** (Sheep) | **~tal** / **~pa** | **Tuutal / Tuupa** | Ram / Tup (Male sheep) |
| **Tuu** (Sheep) | **~pi** / **~nu** | **Tuupi / Tuunu** | Ewe / Yow (Adult female sheep) |
| **Tuu** (Sheep) | **~la** | **Tuula** | Gimmer / Yearling ewe (Young female) |
| **Tuu** (Sheep) | **~no** | **Tuuno** | Lamb (Offspring / Young) |
| **Tuu** (Sheep) | **~sa** | **Tuusa** | Mutton / Lamb meat |
| **Tuu** (Sheep) | **~hon** | **Tuuhon** | Flock / Herd of sheep |
| **Tuu** (Sheep) | **~honpi** | **Tuuhonpi** | Large flock / Great herd of sheep |
| **Mi** (Human/Person) | **~hon** | **Mihon** | Crowd / Group / Band of people |
| **Mi** (Human/Person) | **~honpi** | **Mihonpi** | Multitude / Throng / Horde of people |

> **Note on `~pi`:** In conversational usage, appending `~pi` to an animal or object does not always mean "female"—it frequently acts as an augmentative suffix meaning "large," "grown," or "main."

## 5. Morphophonemics & Abstract Noun Formation (`~na`)

### Negation (`~lo`) & Confirmation (`~mah`)
* **~lo**: Negates an adjective/verb (*Hoihlo* = Bad, *Damlo* = Sick).
* **~mah**: Confirms or agrees with a state (*Hoihmah* = It is indeed good).

### Morphophonemic Spelling Rules with `~na`
Appending **~na** creates abstract nouns, triggering phonetic shifts:
* **Hoih** (Good) $\rightarrow$ **Hoihna** (Goodness)
  * *Negated:* **Hoihlohna** (Badness / Lack of goodness) — *Note the insertion of `-h` on `lo` $\rightarrow$ `loh`.*
* **Dam** (Healthy) $\rightarrow$ **Damna** (Health)
  * *Negated:* **Damlohna** (Sickness / Illness)
* **Hamsia** (Unfortunate) $\rightarrow$ **Hamsiatna** (Misfortune) — *Note the insertion of `-t` on `sia` $\rightarrow$ `siat`.*

Writing *"hoihlona"* or *"hamsiana"* is grammatically incorrect in native usage.

### Anatomic Terms & Medical Expressions (`~na` vs. `Natna`)
* **Body Part + `~na`** = Localized Pain (*Guhna* = Bone pain, *Khena* = Foot pain, *Lutangna* = Headache).
* **Body Part + `Natna`** = Specific Disease/Condition (*Guh natna* = Bone disease).

## 6. Comprehensive Expressive Adjectives Dictionary Matrix (Ideophones)

Zolai expressive adjectives follow a bimoric template $C_1 V_1 C_2 + C_1 V_2 C_2$. Their semantic weight is governed by vowel height and tongue position:
* **Light / Front Vowels (`e`, `i`):** Affectionate, playful, diminutive, toddler-friendly.
* **Heavy / Back Vowels (`a`, `u`, `o`):** Critical, heavy, harsh, messy, severe.
* **Dynamic Ablaut (`e-a`, `i-u`):** Visual/dynamic state.

### A. Motion, Swaying & Posture Domain

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`l_ng`** | **Lengleng** | **Lenglang** | **Langlang** | *Lengleng:* Gentle floating/sweet closeness. *Langlang:* Awkwardly swinging, oversized, unkempt. |

## 7. Structural Morphology Particles & Example Phrases

| Particle | Grammatical Function | English Equivalent | Focus & Tone |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **cin** | Manner Adverbial | *"in a ... manner"* / *"-ly"* | How an action/state is executed. |
| **cia** | Circumstantial Condition | *"while being in a state of..."* | Ongoing condition during an action (short form of *ci in ah*). |
| **laizang** | Emphatic Focus / Absolute State | *"in the absolute state of..."* | High-intensity emphasis on a condition. |
| **teh / leh** | Conditional / Comparative | *"if..."* / *"when compared"* | Sets a condition; *teh* is specifically used for things improving/maturing over time (e.g., wine, fruit). |
| **vehaw** | Exclamatory / Evidential | *"look how..."* / *"indeed!"* | Draws immediate attention to an observation. |
| **lua** | Degree Intensifier | *"too..."* / *"excessively"* | Expresses excessiveness/severity. |
| **luateh / luain** | Extreme Condition | *"since it is so..."* | *luateh* for self/group action, *luain* for third-person reference. |
| **thapai** | Habitual Continuative | *"persistently..."* | Indicates an unceasing, repeated state. |### Corrected Structural Examples
#### 1. Acoustic Pattern (`pengpang` – Metallic Clatter / Exposed State)
* **Pengpang cin:** *Pengpang cin asat uhi.* / *Amau in pengpang cin asat uhi.* (They are striking/hammering it in a loud, jarring manner.)
* **Pengpang cia:** *Pengpang cia!* / *Sik pengpang cia!* (In that loud, clattering state! / While making that jarring noise!)
* **Pengpang laizang:** *Pualam ah kisa pengpang laizang hi!* (They lie outside in plain view in an absolute state of messy, exposed disorder!)
* **Pengpang teh:** *Aging pengpang teh, ka lutang ana hi.* (When the sound is so loudly clattering, my head hurts.)
* **Pengpang vehaw:** *Tua sik pengpang vehaw!* / *Tua sik phei pengpang vehaw!* (Look at how that metal is clattering / look at how jarring that step is!)
* **Pengpang lua:** *Hih aging pengpang lua hi, khawl in.* (This sound is far too jarring and clattering; stop it.)
* **Pengpang luateh / luain:** 
  * Group action: *Ging pengpang luateh, apualam ah taisan nung.* (Since it is making such a loud noise, let's leave for the outside.)
  * Third-person action: *Ging pengpang luain, apualam ah taisan ni.* (Because it makes such a loud noise, let's leave them behind and go outside.)
* **Pengpang thapai:** *Sun leh zan in pengpang thapai uhi.* (Day and night, they persistently make that loud, jarring noise.)
#### 2. Visual Posture Pattern (`kengkang` – Stiff / Awkward Gait)
* **Kengkang:** *Amah apai kengkang hi.* (He walks around in a stiff, awkward posture — verb precedes adjective, no *cin* needed.)
* **Kengkang cia:** *Kengkang cia!* (In that stiff, awkward posture!)
* **Kengkang laizang:** *Hihpa ading kengkang laizang hi!* (This man is standing in an absolute state of rigid, awkward posture!)
* **Kengkang leh:** *Na pai kengkang leh, mite'n hong en ding hi.* (If you walk in such a stiff way, people will stare at you.)
* **Kengkang vehaw:** *Apai dan kengkang vehaw!* (Look at how stiffly and awkwardly he is walking!)
* **Kengkang lua:** *Na din dan kengkang lua, khawl in.* / *Na din dan kengkang lua teh, khawl in.* (Your standing posture is far too stiff; stop it.)
* **Kengkang thapai:** *Sun leh zan in kengkang thapai hi.* (Day and night, he persistently maintains that stiff posture.)
#### 3. Quality Pattern (`hoih` – Good)
* **Hoih takin:** *Na sep te hoih takin sem in.* (Do your work in a good, proper manner — uses *takin* instead of *cin*.)
* **Hoih cia:** *Hoih cia!* (While it is in a good, healthy state!)
* **Hoih laizang:** *Hih van hoih laizang hi!* (This item is in an absolute state of excellence!)
* **Hoih leh / teh:** *Hih pen a hoih leh, lei ni.* (If this one is good, let me buy it. *Hoih teh* is reserved for things that naturally improve/age like wine or fruit.)
* **Hoih vehaw:** *Tua inn hoih vehaw!* (Look at how fine/good that house is!)
* **Hoih lua:** *Hih an hoih lua hi.* (This meal/food is exceptionally good.)
* **Hoih thapai:** *Amah bel sun leh zan in hoih thapai hi.* (He remains persistently good/kind at all times.)

## 8. Orthographic Character Constraints & Conclusion Rules

In native Zolai orthography and traditional composition, the standard Roman alphabet characters **`j`**, **`q`**, **`r`**, **`w`**, **`x`**, and **`y`** are **unused and excluded** from native vocabulary construction.

When rendering loanwords, proper nouns, foreign sounds, or conversational pronunciations that naturally involve these unused character sound values:
1. **Phonetic Substitution:** Native spelling substitutes approximate Zolai character combinations for foreign sounds.
2. **Diacritics & Punctuation:** Apostrophes (`'`), hyphens (`-`), or special punctuation symbols are utilized to indicate glottal stops, tone breaks, or modified vowel lengthenings.
3. **Omission / Ignoring:** The missing character values are made up through native phoneme equivalents, skipped, or ignored entirely in standard Zolai dictionary compositions.
